# Observed patterns from eight reference videos

All of this is OBSERVED: read or counted directly in narration transcripts the user supplied, one to four videos per channel, no performance data. It shows what these creators did, not what works. Learn the pattern; never reuse sentences, analogies or jokes.

References: a roughly 15-minute Hindi video on what a PhD is; a roughly 30-minute Hindi video on how black holes were discovered; a roughly 27-minute Hindi science video by Gaurav Thakur (Get Set Fly) on deep-sea viruses; a roughly 20-minute English video on BlackRock by an Indian creator; four recent Kurzgesagt videos (cosmic voids, heat death, prehistory, the solar-system barrier). The Hindi files are auto-captions with recognition errors. Kurzgesagt is a large studio with a dozen drafts per script, so its scripts are heavily edited.

## Register and wording in the Hindi files

- "Aap" for statements and the viewer, bare imperatives ("socho", "imagine karo", "dekhna") for invitations.
- English for technical nouns and logic words (so, but, actually, eventually), Hindi for emotion and reaction.
- Explicit signposting before the hardest idea.
- Personal sign-off in the Hindi videos; no sign-off line in the four Kurzgesagt videos.

## What to port to the Hinglish space channel (INFERENCE)

1. Hook formula: one flat bold claim, one stake word, one turn, one number or teaser, one explicit promise, in 45 to 75 seconds, with no greeting.
2. Chapter cards written as claims or as the viewer's next question, one every 2 to 4 minutes.
3. A named actor for every thought experiment.
4. Scale ladders with one number per rung and a human-scale conversion each time. Use Indian anchors and lakh and crore, chosen for accuracy first.
5. State the generous assumption, then show why it still fails; or give the loophole its own chapter on whether it truly works.
6. Deflation jokes translated by function, not word for word.
7. Callback endings: reuse the hook's phrase, add one meaning line, then a one-to-three-word beat.
8. Sponsor and promo handling, if the channel ever carries any: a pivot sentence at a question just opened, a return line, a re-tease.
9. Mark speculation out loud before it starts and where evidence ends.

## Differences between the families

Kurzgesagt hooks are shorter, use fewer direct questions and more concrete number ladders, speak as "we", rely on deadpan humour, and have no personal sign-off. The Hindi videos run longer (15 to 30 minutes), use a heavier question chain, more explicit signposting, local analogies and a personal sign-off. Both flip a common belief early, end on a reframe, and mark uncertainty after a dramatic opening.

## Limits

Eight videos, small samples, no retention data, no visuals or music. Hindi counts come from machine captions. Chapter lines are assumed to be on-screen cards. Treat every row as a pattern to test, not a rule.
