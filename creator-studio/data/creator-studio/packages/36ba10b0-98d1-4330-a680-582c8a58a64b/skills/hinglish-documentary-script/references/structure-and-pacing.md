# Structure and pacing

Contents: spine, skeletons by length, word budgets, chapters and transitions, endings, pacing, observed chapter timings.

## The spine: ABT

ABT is And, But, Therefore. State two relevant facts (And), the conflict that makes the topic necessary (But), then the resolution or approach (Therefore). Replace every "and then" with "but" or "therefore" where you can; that is the best-documented structural engine (documented framework, Randy Olson, from Parker and Stone's practice). Use it at three scales: the video's one-sentence promise, each chapter, and a final revision pass that hunts "and then" links.

Pick the shape by topic (INFERENCE):

| Topic type | Shape |
|---|---|
| "Everyone believes X" | Misconception first: state the belief, show why it fails, give the right answer |
| Discovery or history | Detective chain: each clue raises the next question (seen in a 30-minute Hindi black-hole video) |
| Mission or person | Story Circle (You, Need, Go, Search, Find, Take, Return, Change) |
| Argument or analysis | Thesis, roadmap, supporting chapters, restate freshly, zoom out |
| Thought experiment | A named actor goes through stages, each ending on a new obstacle (OBSERVED, Kurzgesagt) |
| Scale | A ladder of rungs, one number each, with human-scale conversions |

Do not encode AIDA, PAS, inverted pyramid or listicle shapes as researched rules; they were not sourced.

## Skeletons by length (INFERENCE: starting points to tune)

### Short, 6 to 8 minutes (about 41 paragraphs in the user's earlier script)

| Segment | Share | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 10% | Claim or scene, stake, turn, promise |
| Context and stakes | 8% | The video-level And, the one-sentence promise |
| Chapters (3 to 5) | 62% | Each one a But then a Therefore; best moment near two-thirds |
| Payoff and "so what" | 12% | Resolve the main loop, one meaning line |
| Close and bridge | 8% | Callback, short punch, at most one ask |

### Mid, 10 to 15 minutes

Hook (45 to 75 s), context (60 to 90 s), 4 to 5 chapters of about 2 to 3.5 minutes, each opened by a question and closed on a new problem, payoff (60 to 90 s), close (30 to 60 s). Add one mid-video re-hook at the deepest point.

### Long, 15 to 30 minutes

Observed in 15 to 30 minute Hindi videos: 5 to 9 chapters of 2 to 4 minutes, a question at the end of each, a cold open of 1 to 3 minutes before a promise, then a title sting. Plan the biggest surprise near two-thirds and one reflective beat in the final third.

## Word budgets at the default speed

## Chapters and transitions

- Each chapter has one idea, stated in one sentence, and must change the viewer's understanding or be cut (the "so what" test).
- Name chapters as claims, jokes or the viewer's next question ("Space Hates You", "Can they actually live forever?"). Chapter titles can double as re-hooks (OBSERVED).
- Close each chapter on a "But": a new complication or an unanswered question. Open the next with the "Therefore" attempt.
- Tease a loop before showing it. A fresh question or surprise at every section boundary is a heuristic; fixed second counts are folklore.
- One motif (an image, phrase or object) introduced in the hook, varied at each re-hook and resolved in the close (INFERENCE; no source covers motif craft).
- "Solve, then new problem" chains: "Great, next stop is...". Then "Oh. Oh no." Each stage ends on a fresh obstacle (OBSERVED).

## Story spine and flow (added after user feedback: "it sounds like someone listing points")

A list of milestones, each with its own chapter, is not a documentary. A documentary has one question and one idea that answers it, and each part exists because the previous one left a problem.

Write the spine first, in five lines:
1. Central question (the viewer's, not the topic's).
2. Through-line idea that answers it, one sentence.
3. Opening scene or person, concrete, with a date or a place.
4. Three to five deep moments that prove the idea. Cut the milestones that do not.
5. The closing image or phrase, which is the hook's own scene or line coming back.

Useful shapes: scene, flashback, return to the scene; a hand-over chain where each part ends on the question the next part answers; a recurring contrast (before and after the idea). For history and "evolution of" topics, organise by the idea changing, not by the calendar.

Flow test: read only the first sentence of every paragraph. Each should follow from the one before by a cause ("isliye"), a turn ("lekin", "par"), or a question. A run of "phir", "ab", "aur" openers means a list. A part that can move elsewhere unchanged is not part of the story.

On-screen cards (a year, a place) are fine; spoken chapter names ("Pehli sardi aayi", "Dhyan hi sab kuch hai") sound like slides and are not read aloud. Connect parts with a spoken sentence instead.

Callbacks that work: a phrase or scene from the hook returning with new meaning (a translation failure from an early chapter returning when a later model beats the same task). Callbacks that fail: a recap, a list of what was covered.

## Endings

Resolve the main loop first, say in one line what is still unknown, place the one soft call to action here, then call back to the hook's own scene or phrase and hand the viewer a final question or image, then a one-to-three-word punch. Never end on the call to action, a recap, a bridge line that is not sourced, or an "ek baat aur" afterthought. All eight reference videos end on a reframe or callback, not a recap (OBSERVED). One soft call to action after the payoff. Use the abrupt style for suspense videos and zoom-out for reflective ones.

## Pacing

- Median sentence 10 to 16 words, occasional long sentence, a few punch beats of four words or fewer after a long build (OBSERVED).
- Question density in the references: 0.3 to 1.5 per minute.
- Keep visuals changing every 5 to 10 seconds as a floor for faceless video; do not lay stock footage over narration just to fill time (CREATOR, Vox alumni).
- Pauses are part of the script: leave room after a major idea. An awe moment is carried by a pause and music, not by adjectives.
